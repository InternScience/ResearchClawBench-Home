# Local Literature Notes

## paper_000.pdf
Halloran et al. (2024) describe GeoH2, a geospatial green-hydrogen model that co-optimizes renewable supply, storage, transport, and conversion chains. The useful benchmark takeaway for this run is methodological rather than numerical: a transparent spatial hydrogen model should separate renewable production, conversion, transport, and infrastructure-access costs.

## paper_001.pdf
Muller et al. (2023) present a least-cost geospatial modeling approach for green hydrogen in Kenya. The paper reinforces three choices used here: hybrid wind-solar supply, explicit siting heterogeneity, and delivered-cost comparison across use cases rather than just plant-gate hydrogen cost.

## paper_002.pdf
Steffen (2020) reviews renewable-energy cost of capital estimation and shows that private WACC differs materially across countries and technologies, with developing countries typically facing higher financing costs. This motivates the benchmark scenarios that vary African WACC separately from European WACC.

## paper_003.pdf
Schmidt et al. (2019) show that rising interest rates can materially reverse renewable-cost gains. This supports the benchmark's high-rate stress case and the claim discipline that financing conditions can reshape competitiveness even when techno-economic assumptions are unchanged.

## Working Interpretation For This Benchmark
The local literature supports a transparent, line-item geospatial cost model with scenario analysis around financing and policy support. Because the benchmark data are minimal and there are no hourly profiles or local port-demand pairs, this run uses a simplified but auditable delivered-cost formulation rather than a full operations optimization.
